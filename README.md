<h2>Advancing the European metrology for wideband AC voltage</h2>

<h3>Overview</h3>
During recent years, the performance of equipment for the measurement of alternating current (AC) voltage at frequencies up to 100 MHz has improved considerably, but the supporting metrology lags behind the market. Novel wideband AC voltage measurement standards and sources play a crucial role in disseminating wideband AC voltage traceability from the primary level to calibration laboratories and industry and there is no National Metrology Institute (NMI) in Europe capable of fully calibrating this equipment. This project will facilitate the development and validation of novel primary radio frequency direct current (RF-DC) standards, improved measurement methods and setups for AC voltage in the range 1 mV up to 50 V and at frequencies up to 100 MHz, including the provision of software for control and data processing, through collaborative research activities to enhance traceable calibration services in this field.
<h3>Objectives</h3>
The overall objective is to develop a traceable regional/national metrology capability in wideband AC voltage up to 100 MHz. 
The specific objectives of the project are:

1.	To develop novel RF-DC standards based on the principle of calorimetric thermal voltage converters at input voltages from 1 V up to 5 V and frequencies up to 100 MHz with target uncertainties (k=1) better than 10 μV/V at 1 MHz and 500 μV/V at 100 MHz.

2.	To investigate and extend the step-down and step-up measurement methods based on thermal transfer techniques for frequencies up to 100 MHz, scaling from the basic voltage level of primary RF DC standards (1 V up to 5 V) down to 1 mV and up to 50 V.

3.	To develop traceable methods for providing conversion between impedance mismatched RF-DC standards and impedance matched (50 Ω) wideband measuring instruments at frequencies up to 100 MHz, including investigations of the connections, connectors, cables, transmission line effects, hardware performance, grounding conditions including analysis of uncertainty contributions.

4.	To develop, validate and provide at least two new working setups based on the novel RF DC standards, extended step-up and step-down measurement methods, and a traceable method for providing conversion between impedance mismatched and matched measurement. The targeted voltage range will be 1 mV up to 50 V at frequencies up to 100 MHz with the expanded uncertainties (k=2) of 200 μV/V (1 MHz) to 5 mV/V (100 MHz) at 1 mV, and of 100 μV/V (1 MHz) to 2.5 mV/V (100 MHz) at 50 V. To also develop an open software tool to operate the setup, control the hardware and evaluate the results.

5.	To facilitate take up and long-term operation of the capabilities, technology and measurement infrastructure for AC voltage measurement developed in the project, by the measurement supply chain (NMIs/DIs, calibration and testing laboratories), and end users (e.g. industry, instrument manufacturers, regulators). The approach will be discussed within the consortium and with other EURAMET NMIs/DIs, e.g., via EURAMET TC-EM and the EMN for Smart Electricity Grids (SEG), to ensure that a coordinated and optimised development of traceability in this field is developed for Europe as a whole.

<h3>Project outcomes</h3>
Throughout the project lifecycle, results will be made publicly available on GitHub as they are developed.
